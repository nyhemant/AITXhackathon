About

KidZooKit is an at-home virtual zoo (and aquarium, museum, and park) for curious kids — something to open on a phone or laptop when you have a little time together. A printable hunt is optional if you’re going in person. Questions or a venue to suggest? arku2arku@gmail.com

Arya and Kunal go to Lamplighter School, and wildlife questions never really stop at our house. A dad built KidZooKit so they — and other busy families — could watch a cam, flip through a few cards, or plan a short visit without turning curiosity into a project. It’s free, works on a phone, and stays small on purpose. If it gives your kids the same spark, that’s why it’s here.

Common questions

What should I see at the zoo with a toddler?

Pick a few big, easy animals — elephants, giraffes, penguins — instead of trying to cover the whole zoo. KidZooKit gives you that short list on the place page — talk it through at home, or print a hunt if you’re going.

How does the treasure hunt work?

A full session works on a phone or laptop with no printer. Going in person? Optionally print a one-page hunt, bring a pen, and check things off as you walk. No scores. Paper is a companion, not required.

Is it free?

Everything on KidZooKit is free. Tickets, passes, and parking are up to each zoo, aquarium, museum, or park.

We’re tourists / visiting for one day — is this for us?

Open the place page the night before (hotel or home). Do a short session first, then optionally print a hunt so you’re not wandering until everyone’s exhausted. Starter lists are basic; hand-picked kits note when the list was last checked.

Do you have national park scavenger hunts?

U.S. and international parks are on the Parks tab (and national-parks). Each kit covers one finishable slice (for example Old Faithful boardwalks or Lake Louise shore — not the whole park), with a short stop list and printable mission. Switch the map to International for parks outside the U.S.

Can teachers or homeschool groups use this?

Families first — and it works for teachers and small groups too. Use the at-home cards and session together; print is optional for a group visit. Free, no accounts. Handy for small field trips when you want a simple mission instead of a free-for-all map.

Can we do this at home with no printer?

Open a place page or Watch Live on a phone or laptop. Print is optional if you’re going in person.

Which cities are covered?

Kits cover dozens of U.S. cities (Dallas–Fort Worth, Chicago, New York, San Diego, and more) and major places worldwide (London, Singapore, Tokyo, Sydney, and others). Visitors in the U.S. usually start on the U.S. map; elsewhere we open the world map. You can always switch with Popular, US, or International.
