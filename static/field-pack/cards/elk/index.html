Quick tips (Junior Ranger) — tap to open
  • Big deer of forests, meadows, and mountain edges
  • Pale rump patch that stands out
  • Bulls grow large antlers each year, then shed them
  • Live in herds (cows & calves often together)
  • Also called wapiti
1

Forest-meadow home

Where do elk like to live in the wild?

Elk dwell in open forest and forest-edge habitats. They also use mountain meadows and forest openings.

2

Rump patch

What pale mark helps an elk stand out from behind?

Elk are also called wapiti, from a word meaning “white rump.” They have a distinctive light-colored rump patch.

3

Antlers

What happens to a bull elk’s antlers each year?

Male elk grow large antlers and routinely shed them each year as the weather warms, then grow a new set.

4

Graze and browse

What do elk eat?

Elk graze on grasses and sedges and also browse higher plants — leaves, twigs, and bark.

5

Herd life

How do elk herds usually work?

Elk are very social. For most of the year, adult males and females live in different herds. Female herds are larger; bulls form small groups or travel alone, then come together in breeding season.

6

Bugle

What is an elk bugle?

Bulls bugle in the mating season — a loud series of throaty whistles, bellows, and other calls that can travel a long way.

7

Spotted calves

What do new elk calves often look like?

Calves are born spotted, as is common with many deer species, and they lose those spots by the end of summer.

8

Soft forest care

How should people care around wild elk and their home?

Wikipedia ties elk to forest, meadow, and mountain-edge homes. Soft care is space plus a healthy habitat — no status letter.

9

Big deer

How big are elk among deer?

The elk is the second-largest living deer, after the moose.

10

Myth buster

Is a North American elk the same animal Europeans call “elk”?

The word “elk” first meant the European moose. North American colonists used “elk” for this deer, also called wapiti.

Facts from Wikipedia, Elk. Where sources disagree on exact numbers, we keep them approximate.
