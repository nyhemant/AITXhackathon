🐟 River / lake fish

Fish that live in fresh water — rivers, lakes, ponds.

1

Fresh homes

Where do freshwater fish live?

Wikipedia says freshwater fish spend some or all of their lives in rivers, lakes, ponds, and inland wetlands — not salty ocean water.

2

Low-salt water

How is fresh water different from ocean water?

Wikipedia says freshwater homes have very low salt compared with the ocean. That low-salt water is what “fresh” means here.

3

Gill breathers

How do freshwater fish get oxygen underwater?

Wikipedia says fish extract oxygen from water using gills. Freshwater fish breathe underwater this way.

4

Fin toolkit

How do fins help freshwater fish move?

Wikipedia describes a typical fish with paired fins plus dorsal, anal, and tail fins. Those fins help push, steer, balance, and stop.

5

Many kinds

How many kinds of fish live in fresh water?

Wikipedia says a huge share of all known fish kinds are found in fresh water. The exact percent can shift, so we keep the number soft.

6

Scaly coats

Do freshwater fish wear scales?

Wikipedia says a typical fish has skin covered with scales that help protect it. Some kinds have fewer scales or none, so this is not true of every freshwater fish.

7

Water temperature

How do most freshwater fish handle temperature?

Wikipedia says most fish are cold-blooded — their body temperature varies with the surrounding water. A few kinds can stay warmer, so we say most.

8

Egg starts

How do many freshwater fish begin life?

Wikipedia says many fish begin as eggs laid in the water. Some kinds give live young, so we stay soft.

9

Soft water care

What helps freshwater fish stay healthy in the wild?

Freshwater fish need clean fresh water. Wikipedia notes that habitat loss and pollution can hurt them. Clean rivers and healthy wetlands help them thrive. We do not lock a status letter — that grade is a snapshot and can change.

10

Myth buster

Do all fish need the ocean to live?

Wikipedia says freshwater fish spend some or all of their lives in rivers, lakes, ponds, and inland wetlands. Plenty of kinds never need the ocean.

Talk about it

  1. Most familiar freshwater fish are ray-finned. How is that different from everyday “fish”?
  2. Special gill cells called ionocytes pump salts in. Why would a pond fish need that?
  3. Many salmon swim up to fresh water to breed; many eels swim down to the sea. What’s the difference?

Push further

  1. Some swim bladders open to the gut; others are closed. How might a fish add or lose air?
  2. Isolated lakes can act like islands. Why might that make more kinds of fish?
  3. An IUCN snapshot grades kinds one by one. Why not give the whole group one letter?
