1

Two front claws

How many big curved claws does a two-toed sloth have on each front foot?

Wikipedia says two-toed sloths have two claws on each front foot. People sometimes call them two-fingered sloths. Those claws help them hang.

2

Rainforest home

Where do two-toed sloths live in the wild?

Wikipedia places two-toed sloths in tropical forests of Central and South America. They live in the trees, not on ice or African grassland.

3

Upside-down life

How do two-toed sloths spend most of their lives?

Wikipedia says two-toed sloths spend most of their lives hanging upside down. They sleep, eat, and rest that way.

4

Slow savers

Why do two-toed sloths move so slowly?

Wikipedia says they move at an extremely slow rate. A low-energy lifestyle helps them save energy. They do have legs — they hang and pull along.

5

Algae camouflage

Why can a two-toed sloth’s fur look a little green?

Wikipedia says greenish algae can grow in the fur and help them blend in. That camouflage helps them hide in the canopy.

6

Leaf snacks

What do two-toed sloths mostly eat?

Wikipedia says they eat leaves and other plant parts such as shoots and fruit. They are not pizza-eaters or fish-only hunters.

7

Night roamers

When are two-toed sloths mostly active?

Wikipedia says two-toed sloths are predominantly nocturnal. They rest quietly by day and move more at night.

8

Hook hangers

How do their long curved claws help them?

Wikipedia says they hang using long, curved claws. Those hooks lock onto branches. They do not fly.

9

Soft forest care

Why do two-toed sloths need healthy rainforest trees?

Two-toed sloths live in rainforest trees. Healthy trees give them leaves to eat and places to hide. We do not lock a status letter — that grade is a snapshot and can change.

10

Myth buster

Are two-toed sloths just lazy?

Wikipedia describes their slow, low-energy lifestyle. Slow is not “lazy” — it saves energy and helps them stay hard to spot.
