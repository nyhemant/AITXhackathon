Quick tips (Junior Ranger) — tap to open
  • Wild Sumatran tigers live only on the island of Sumatra.
  • They are among the smallest living tigers.
  • Their stripes are extra busy, with little spots between the stripes.
  • They hunt in forest, not open grassland like lions.
  • They eat meat — deer, wild pigs, and other forest prey.
1

Sumatra only

Where do wild Sumatran tigers live?

Wikipedia says the Sumatran tiger lives on the Indonesian island of Sumatra — not all of Asia.

2

Smallest living tiger

How does a Sumatran tiger’s size compare with other living tigers?

Wikipedia calls the Sumatran tiger one of the smallest tigers. We do not lock one weight.

3

Busy stripes

What is special about a Sumatran tiger’s stripes?

Wikipedia says Sumatran tigers have more stripes than other tigers, and lines of small dark spots sit between the regular stripes.

4

Forest cat

What kind of place do Sumatran tigers prefer?

Wikipedia says they strongly prefer forest with dense cover. They are not open-grassland cats like lions.

5

Meat hunter

What do wild Sumatran tigers hunt?

Wikipedia lists forest prey such as sambar deer, muntjac, and wild pigs (banded pigs).

6

Mostly alone

How do Sumatran tigers usually live?

Wikipedia’s Tiger page says a tiger lives a mostly solitary life — mostly alone, not pride life like lions.

7

Cub

What do you call a baby Sumatran tiger?

A baby tiger is a cub — the same word used for a baby lion.

8

Male ruff

What extra fur can a male Sumatran tiger show?

Wikipedia says males have a prominent ruff, and it is especially marked in the Sumatran tiger.

9

Last Sunda tiger

Why is the Sumatran tiger called the last Sunda island tiger?

Wikipedia says it is the only surviving tiger population in the Sunda Islands after the Bali and Javan tigers went extinct.

10

Myth buster — water

Are Sumatran tigers scared of water, like a cartoon house cat?

Wikipedia’s Tiger page says the tiger is a powerful swimmer and readily uses water, especially on hot days — not a water-scared cartoon cat.

Facts from Wikipedia, Sumatran tiger. Where sources disagree on exact numbers, we keep them approximate.
