1

River horse

What does the name hippopotamus mean?

Wikipedia says the name comes from Ancient Greek for “horse of the river.”

2

Day and night

What do Nile hippos usually do by day and at dusk?

Hippos remain cool by staying in water or mud during the day, then come out at dusk to graze.

3

Grass, not fish

What do wild Nile hippos mostly eat?

A wild hippo’s diet is almost entirely grass, with only a little aquatic plant matter — not fish.

4

Periscope face

Why are a Nile hippo’s eyes, ears, and nostrils high on its head?

Those parts sit high on the skull so they can stay above the water while the rest of the body is submerged.

5

“Blood sweat”

What is the reddish liquid people call a hippo’s “blood sweat”?

People nickname it “blood sweat,” but it is neither blood nor sweat. It is a red-colored sunscreen secretion.

6

Surprise cousins

Who are a hippo’s closest living relatives?

Hippos can look a bit like pigs, but their closest living relatives are whales and dolphins.

7

Huge yawn

What does a hippo’s huge “yawn” usually mean?

The big “yawn” is a threat display. Tusks are mainly for fighting. Hippos pull grass with their lips, not those long teeth.

8

Stay back

Why do people treat wild hippos with extra care?

Hippos are strong and can be aggressive if they are surprised or protecting young. Give them space.

9

Water babies

Where do Nile hippos often have babies, and how can calves drink milk?

Mating and birth often occur in water. Calves can nurse underwater. Mothers stay very protective of their young.

10

Wet skin

Why does a Nile hippo need water or mud on its skin?

Hippos have little hair. Their thick skin still needs water or mud, or it can crack even with the sunscreen goo.
