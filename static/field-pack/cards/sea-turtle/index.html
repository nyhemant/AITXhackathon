1

Seven kinds

How many kinds of sea turtle live in the world’s oceans, if we keep the list soft?

Wikipedia names seven living sea turtles: flatback, green, hawksbill, leatherback, loggerhead, Kemp’s ridley, and olive ridley. We keep that list light — no deep family-tree names here.

2

Flippers

What do a sea turtle’s flippers do?

Wikipedia describes sea turtles as ocean reptiles with paddle flippers, not the column-like feet of many land turtles. Front flippers power the swim; hind flippers help steer and later dig the nest.

3

Streamlined shell

How is a sea turtle’s shell built for the ocean?

Wikipedia says sea turtles have a more fusiform, streamlined body than many land or freshwater turtles. That flatter, smoother shell cuts drag so they can swim more easily.

4

Beach nest

How does a mom sea turtle make a nest?

Wikipedia says the nesting female hauls onto a sandy beach, nearly always at night, and digs a hole with her hind flippers. She buries the clutch, smooths the sand, and returns to the ocean.

5

Soft eggs

What are sea turtle eggs like — and does mom stay to guard them?

Wikipedia says she fills the nest with soft-shelled eggs, covers them, and leaves them untended. Mom does not stay to guard the clutch.

6

Hatchling run

What do baby sea turtles do after they hatch?

Wikipedia says hatchlings in one nest emerge together, then crawl toward the brightest horizon — traditionally the ocean, where moonlight and starlight shine on the water. Most kinds hatch at night.

7

Air breathers

How do sea turtles breathe, even though they live in the ocean?

Wikipedia says sea turtles are air-breathing reptiles with lungs, so they surface to breathe. A resting turtle can stay under a long time; exact hours shift, so we keep that time soft.

8

Ocean homes

Where do sea turtles live?

Wikipedia finds sea turtles in all oceans except the polar regions. They live in the sea; none of the seven kinds lives only on land.

9

Long trips

How far can many sea turtles travel, if we keep the miles soft?

Wikipedia says many sea turtles migrate long distances between feeding waters and nesting beaches — some even cross ocean basins. Exact miles vary, so we keep that travel story soft.

10

Myth buster

Can a sea turtle pull its head and flippers into its shell like many pet turtles?

Wikipedia says the streamlined body means sea turtles cannot retract their head and limbs into the shell, unlike many other turtles and tortoises. The shape is built for swimming, not hiding inside.
