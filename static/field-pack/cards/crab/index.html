Quick tips (Junior Ranger) — tap to open
  • Hard-shelled animals with claws
  • Ten-legged crustaceans (decapods)
  • Many walk sideways (“crabwise”)
  • Live in oceans, shores — some in fresh water or on land
  • Must shed their shell to grow
1

Hard shell

What covers a crab’s body?

Wikipedia says crabs have a heavily armoured shell — a thick exoskeleton that covers and protects the body.

2

Claws

What do a crab’s front legs often end in?

Wikipedia says true crabs often have claws. The front legs often end in pincers used for eating and defense.

3

Sideways

How do many crabs move?

Wikipedia says many crabs can run swiftly sideways (“crabwise”), and they often hide in rocky crevices. Some walk forward, so we say many.

4

Hide

Where do many crabs like to hide?

Wikipedia says crabs have a habit of hiding in rocky crevices. Many also use sand and shore tide pools as hideouts.

5

Molt

How does a crab grow bigger?

Wikipedia’s moulting notes say crustaceans shed the old exoskeleton so they can grow. The new shell starts soft and then hardens.

6

Many kinds

Do all crabs look the same size and shape?

Wikipedia says crabs vary from tiny pea crabs to giant spider crabs, with many shapes and sizes.

7

Ocean to land

Where can crabs live?

Wikipedia says many crabs are marine, and a substantial number live in fresh water or other non-marine homes. Some even live on land.

8

Eat mix

What do many crabs eat?

Wikipedia says many crabs are free-living marine omnivores, eating a mix of algae, scraps, and small animals. Some kinds specialize, so we say many.

9

Soft care

How can people help crabs?

Wikipedia ties crabs to rocky crevices, shores, and reefs. Soft care is keeping those hide-and-grow homes healthy — we do not lock one status letter.

10

Myth buster

Are horseshoe crabs true crabs?

Wikipedia says horseshoe crabs are not true crabs. They belong with a different group of animals, closer to spiders than to true crabs.

Facts from Wikipedia, Crab. Where sources disagree on exact numbers, we keep them approximate.
