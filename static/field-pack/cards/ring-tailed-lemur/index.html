Quick tips (Junior Ranger) — tap to open
  • Wild ring-tailed lemurs live only on Madagascar.
  • They are named for a long black-and-white striped tail.
  • They live in a troop led by females.
  • They sunbathe with their bellies toward the sun to warm up.
  • They mostly eat plants — fruit, leaves, and flowers.
1

Madagascar only

Where do wild ring-tailed lemurs live?

Wild ring-tailed lemurs live only on Madagascar. They are not mainland African or Antarctic animals.

2

Stripy tail

Why are they called ring-tailed lemurs?

The ring-tailed lemur is named for its long black-and-white ringed tail. That stripy tail is the name hook.

3

Girls first

Who leads a ring-tailed lemur troop?

Ring-tailed lemurs live in a troop and females are in charge, including first pick of food.

4

Sun pose

How do ring-tailed lemurs warm up in the morning?

Ring-tailed lemurs sit upright and turn their soft white bellies toward the sun to warm up. That morning sun pose is a group warm-up.

5

Plant snacks

What do ring-tailed lemurs mostly eat?

The diet is mostly plants such as fruit, leaves, and flowers. Extra snacks can show up, but plants come first. We do not lock exact shares.

6

Tail flags

Why do ring-tailed lemurs hold their tails high?

The long tail is not for grabbing branches. Troops hold tails high like flags so everyone can stay together.

7

Ground time

How do ring-tailed lemurs spend a lot of their day, unlike many other lemurs?

Ring-tailed lemurs spend a lot of time on the ground. That is unusual for many other lemurs, which stay in the trees more.

8

Smell marks

How do ring-tailed lemurs share “our place” with the troop?

Ring-tailed lemurs leave scent on trees, rocks, and other objects to mark their place. Smell is a big way the troop shares news.

9

Soft care

What do wild ring-tailed lemur forests need?

Wild forests in Madagascar are under pressure from habitat loss. Those forests need care. We do not lock a status letter — that grade is a snapshot and can change.

10

Myth buster — monkey?

Is a ring-tailed lemur a monkey?

Wikipedia places the ring-tailed lemur with Madagascar’s lemurs, not with monkeys. Sharing a long tail does not make it a monkey.

Facts from Wikipedia, Ring-tailed lemur. Where sources disagree on exact numbers, we keep them approximate.
