1

Madagascar only

Where do wild ring-tailed lemurs live?

Wikipedia says wild ring-tailed lemurs live only on Madagascar. They are not mainland African or Antarctic animals.

2

Stripy tail

Why are they called ring-tailed lemurs?

Wikipedia says the ring-tailed lemur is named for its long black-and-white ringed tail. That stripy tail is the name hook.

3

Girls first

Who leads a ring-tailed lemur troop?

Wikipedia says ring-tailed lemurs live in a troop and females are in charge, including first pick of food. We keep exact troop counts soft.

4

Sun pose

How do ring-tailed lemurs warm up in the morning?

Wikipedia says ring-tailed lemurs sit upright and turn their soft white bellies toward the sun to warm up. That morning sun pose is a group warm-up.

5

Plant snacks

What do ring-tailed lemurs mostly eat?

Wikipedia says the diet is mostly plants such as fruit, leaves, and flowers. Extra snacks can show up, but plants come first. We do not lock exact shares.

6

Tail flags

Why do ring-tailed lemurs hold their tails high?

Wikipedia says the long tail is not for grabbing branches. Troops hold tails high like flags so everyone can stay together.

7

Ground time

How do ring-tailed lemurs spend a lot of their day, unlike many other lemurs?

Wikipedia says ring-tailed lemurs spend a lot of time on the ground. That is unusual for many other lemurs, which stay in the trees more.

8

Smell marks

How do ring-tailed lemurs share “our place” with the troop?

Wikipedia says ring-tailed lemurs leave scent on trees, rocks, and other objects to mark their place. Smell is a big way the troop shares news.

9

Soft care

What do wild ring-tailed lemur forests need?

Wikipedia says wild forests in Madagascar are under pressure from habitat loss. Those forests need care. We do not lock a status letter — that grade is a snapshot and can change.

10

Myth buster — monkey?

Is a ring-tailed lemur a monkey?

Wikipedia places the ring-tailed lemur with Madagascar’s lemurs, not with monkeys. Sharing a long tail does not make it a monkey.
