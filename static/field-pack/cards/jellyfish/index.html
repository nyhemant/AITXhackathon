1

Not fish

Are jellyfish a kind of fish?

Wikipedia places jellyfish in phylum Cnidaria with corals and anemones. They have no backbone, so they are not fish.

2

Bell

What is the soft umbrella-shaped part of a jelly?

Wikipedia says a jellyfish has an umbrella-shaped main body made of mesoglea, known as the bell.

3

Tentacles

What hang below a jelly’s bell?

Wikipedia says trailing tentacles hang on the underside of the bell. They help catch food and sense the water.

4

Sting

What do a jelly’s tentacles use to grab food?

Wikipedia says the tentacles are armed with stinging cells used to capture prey. Many everyday stings feel mild to people, though some can hurt — we stay soft.

5

Pulse swim

How does a jellyfish usually swim?

Wikipedia says pulsating contractions of the bell push the animal through open water.

6

Mostly water

How much of a jelly’s body is water, if we keep the share soft?

Wikipedia says the jelly-like mesoglea is about 95% water or more. Exact shares can shift, so we keep that number soft.

7

Two stages

What two stages do many jellies pass through?

Wikipedia describes a two-stage path: a tiny polyp can live on the seafloor, then a swimming medusa — the jelly we usually see.

8

Everywhere

Where do jellyfish live?

Wikipedia says jellyfish are found all over the world, from surface waters to the deep sea. A few tiny kinds live in fresh water.

9

Soft care

How can people help jellies and the shores they visit?

Wikipedia ties jellies to coasts and open water, and notes that big bloom swarms can bother beaches and fishing. Soft care is clean coasts and balanced seas — we do not lock one status letter.

10

Myth buster

Is a Portuguese man o’ war a true jellyfish?

Wikipedia treats the Portuguese man o’ war as a floating colony of many tiny animals working as one — not a single true jellyfish.

Talk about it

  1. Acraspeda groups true, box, and stalked jellies. How is Hydrozoa the diverse sister branch?
  2. Rhopalia on the bell edge hold eyes and pacemakers. Why might that help a jelly steer?
  3. Warm water, extra nutrients, and fewer fish can favor jelly blooms. Why keep that ecology soft?

Push further

  1. Corals and anemones never swim as medusae. Why might Anthozoa have skipped that stage?
  2. Turritopsis can reverse from medusa to polyp in the lab. Why isn’t every jelly “immortal”?
  3. When fish crash, jellies can sit near the top of the food web. Why keep that “top predator” story soft?
