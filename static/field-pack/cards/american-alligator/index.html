1

Wetland home

Where do American alligators live in the wild?

Wikipedia says American alligators are native to the Southeastern United States. They live in freshwater wetlands such as marshes, swamps, lakes, and slow rivers.

2

Tail motor

How do American alligators power their swimming?

Wikipedia says they swim like fish, moving their pelvic regions and tails from side to side. That strong tail is the motor.

3

Armor plates

What covers an American alligator’s back?

Wikipedia’s crocodilian-armor and scute pages describe bony plates (scutes) along the back. Those plates help protect the alligator.

4

Big bites

What do American alligators use their strong jaws for?

Wikipedia says they eat fish, reptiles, birds, and more. Strong jaws help them catch that wetland prey. We keep size claims soft.

5

Bellow talk

Why do American alligators bellow?

Wikipedia says they bellow to declare territory and locate mates. A loud bellow helps claim space and find a partner.

6

Nest mounds

How do alligator moms make a nest?

Wikipedia says the female builds a nest of vegetation, sticks, leaves, and mud in a sheltered spot near water.

7

Baby bands

What do baby alligators often look like when they hatch?

Wikipedia says young are born with yellow bands around their bodies. Moms help move hatchlings to the water.

8

Gator holes

What can a gator hole do in dry times?

Wikipedia says alligator holes can hold water in the dry season and give other animals a wet place to wait.

9

Soft wetland care

Why do healthy freshwater wetlands matter?

Wikipedia ties alligators to freshwater wetlands for living, nesting, and food. We stay with that soft care — no status letter.

10

Myth buster

Do alligator moms leave their eggs like many reptiles?

Wikipedia says parental care is unusual among most reptiles. Alligator moms guard nests and carry hatchlings to the water.
