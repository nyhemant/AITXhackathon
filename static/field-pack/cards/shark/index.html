1

Cartilage skeleton

What are a shark’s “bones” made of?

Wikipedia says sharks have a skeleton made of cartilage, not bone. Cartilage is strong but bendy — not the hard bone in a dog or a person.

2

Sandpaper skin

Why can shark skin feel rough, like sandpaper?

Wikipedia says shark skin is covered in tiny tooth-like scales called dermal denticles. They can make the skin feel rough.

3

Ocean home

Where do most sharks live?

Wikipedia says sharks live in all the world’s seas. A few kinds can enter rivers, but most do not live in fresh water.

4

Meat or fish

What do most sharks eat?

Wikipedia says most sharks eat meat or fish. A few kinds filter tiny food from the water, so diets differ by kind.

5

Gill slits

How do sharks breathe?

Wikipedia says sharks breathe with gills. They have a row of gill slits on each side of the head — often about five to seven.

6

Swishy swimmers

How is a shark’s body built for moving?

Wikipedia describes sharks as strong swimmers with a powerful body and fins. The Field Trip Kit card calls that a strong swishy body.

7

New teeth

What happens to a shark’s teeth over time?

Wikipedia says sharks grow teeth in the gums and replace them again and again throughout life.

8

Electric sense

What extra sense can sharks use to find other animals?

Wikipedia says sharks can sense the weak electric fields made by other animals. How those tiny sensors work comes later.

9

Soft care

How are sharks doing in the wild today?

Wikipedia says fishing and finning can hurt many kinds of sharks. How each kind is doing is different — we do not lock one status letter.

10

Myth buster

Are most sharks the movie “man-eaters” people imagine?

Wikipedia says movies make sharks look like man-eaters, but of hundreds of kinds, most are not dangerous to people.
